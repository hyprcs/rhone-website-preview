Hypothetical winter growth possibilities

Could household heating services and planned commercial maintenance create more worthwhile work during winter? Start with Sam’s existing expertise and relationships, then test demand and capacity.

Reverse-cycle air conditioning provides heating as well as cooling, making it a possible starting point where Sam already handles suitable systems. Australian Government guidance.

Household heating services

The opportunity: a defined service for suitable existing systems, covering heating operation, appropriate maintenance, operating advice and a findings report. Sam sets the scope; repairs receive a separate quote.

Where Will could help: a clear offer page or Facebook post, a booking request, equipment records, visits grouped by area and a next-service reminder process.

Real example: Cold Front publishes seasonal servicing options and annual-maintenance registration.

Planned commercial maintenance

The opportunity: agreed visits for suitable refrigeration and air-conditioning equipment, with condition reports and separately approved repairs. Frequency follows the actual site and equipment.

Where Will could help: an equipment register, service calendar, tailored proposal and a form that turns job notes and photos into a customer report.

Real example: Eco Air Services offers combined maintenance, asset records and condition reporting.

Heating upgrades where needed

The opportunity: help customers with uncomfortable rooms or ageing equipment understand suitable heating options after an assessment.

Where Will could help: an enquiry form collecting equipment details and photos, clearer comparison quotes, useful heating guides and follow-up that respects the current quote status.

Real example: ComfyHome documents a Melbourne gas-heating replacement with a reverse-cycle multi-split system. Local incentives and Sam’s capability would need separate checks.

Property-manager coordination

The opportunity: where suitable relationships exist, organise service visits across several properties with clear access arrangements and repair approvals.

Where Will could help: one status list showing what is booked, what is complete, access issues and repairs awaiting approval, alongside consistent reports.

Real example: Eco Air’s property services include access coordination and scheduling across properties. This is a possible adaptation to discuss.

A small first trial

  1. Find the actual winter gap. Review available job records, costs, working hours and the weeks Sam wants to fill.
  2. Choose a suitable offer. Investigate household heating servicing and commercial maintenance first; choose the best fit to test.
  3. Prepare the booking and delivery process. Agree the scope, capacity, customer permissions, reports and follow-up.
  4. Review completed work. Track paid jobs, contribution after direct costs and time spent, including travel, administration and trial costs.

Moving existing work from summer to winter can smooth the workload without increasing annual income. Autumn servicing also needs to be distinguished from work that fills June–August.
